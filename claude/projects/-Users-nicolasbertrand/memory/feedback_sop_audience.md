The primary audience of an ISO 13485 SOP is the operator who performs the gesture (Theodo dev, QARA consultant, PM, designer). The NB Technical Assessor is a *downstream check* that the SOP effectively drives the gesture — never the design target.

**Why:** The user pushed back on me framing SOPs as artifacts written for NB assessors. Writing SOPs *for* the NB produces ceremonial documents nobody reads — the audit-shop anti-pattern. In Theodo's Lean posture the gemba (the operator at their workstation) is the customer of every standard, regardless of whether the artifact is labelled "Academy standard" or "SOP." This is what makes one-artifact convergence possible: same audience, same shape.

**How to apply:**
- When designing or critiquing an SOP, optimize first for operator clarity and gesture quality.
- Treat the NB-assessor lens as a downstream sanity check ("would this defensibly evidence the gesture?"), not the target.
- Reject SOP content that paraphrases standards/clauses without telling the operator what to do (reinforces feedback_sop_operational.md).
- Treat the ISO 13485 §4.2.4 metadata wrapper (doc ID, version, signatures, change log, distribution, applicable standards) as required ceremony *around* an operationally-rich body — not as the body itself.
- Do not separate "Academy/Lean standard" from "SOP" on audience grounds. They have the same audience; the difference is metadata wrapper, not content.
